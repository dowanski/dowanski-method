# QDI Discovery Anchors

Status: Candidate operating set  
Purpose: Provide broad discovery coverage without becoming a rigid survey

## How to use the anchors

The 33 anchors identify responsibilities that discovery should address. They do
not require 33 separately spoken answers, sequential delivery, or equal depth.

For each anchor, record one outcome in the Discovery Ledger:

- `ANSWERED` — sufficient for the current decision;
- `EXPAND` — follow-up or a specialist branch is required;
- `SIDEQUEST` — evidence must be gathered outside the conversation;
- `DEFERRED` — intentionally unresolved with an owner and gate;
- `NOT_APPLICABLE` — excluded with a reason;
- `BLOCKING` — the project cannot responsibly advance without resolution.

Do not repeat a question already answered. Cite the earlier answer, verify the
interpretation if needed, and mark the overlapping anchor accordingly.

## A — Intent and ownership

### 01 — Seed

**Ask:** In your own words, what idea, problem, opportunity, or change are we
exploring?

**Protects:** Original meaning before premature solution or technical framing.

### 02 — Reason and timing

**Ask:** Why does this matter to you, and why are you considering it now?

**Protects:** Motivation, urgency, triggering events, and assumed timing.

### 03 — Desired change

**Ask:** If this works, what should become meaningfully different?

**Protects:** The intended change rather than only the imagined artifact.

### 04 — Intent and decision authority

**Ask:** Who holds the original intent, who makes final decisions, and whose
approval or participation can constrain the work?

**Protects:** Ownership and approval without confusing access or operational
roles with business or creative authority.

## B — Current reality and problem

### 05 — Current condition

**Ask:** What exists today, and how is the need currently handled—with or
without a formal solution?

**Protects:** The real baseline and existing workarounds.

### 06 — Friction or opportunity

**Ask:** Where does the current condition create friction, loss, delay, risk,
confusion, or unrealized value?

**Protects:** The experienced problem rather than the requested feature.

### 07 — Evidence of need

**Ask:** What have you observed, measured, heard, or experienced that suggests
this is a real problem or opportunity?

**Protects:** Honest separation of evidence, interpretation, and intuition.

### 08 — Strengths worth preserving

**Ask:** What already works, feels valuable, or should not be lost while the
condition changes?

**Protects:** Useful behavior, trust, and existing strengths.

## C — People and context

### 09 — Direct users or recipients

**Ask:** Who will directly use, receive, experience, or depend on the result?

**Protects:** Distinction among user, buyer, owner, operator, and recipient.

### 10 — User goal

**Ask:** What are those people trying to accomplish before, during, and after
their interaction with this idea?

**Protects:** The wider journey surrounding the proposed solution.

### 11 — Operators and affected parties

**Ask:** Who else must operate, support, approve, supply, integrate with, or
live with the result—even without using its primary interface?

**Protects:** Indirect stakeholders and operational reality.

### 12 — Context and inclusion

**Ask:** Which abilities, access needs, languages, devices, environments,
levels of digital comfort, or real-world conditions could change what a usable
result means?

**Protects:** Accessibility and inclusion as early discovery responsibilities.

## D — Value and alternatives

### 13 — Value

**Ask:** What useful value should this create, and for whom?

**Protects:** Different forms of value for users, operators, owners, customers,
and affected parties.

### 14 — Cost of inaction

**Ask:** What is likely to happen if nothing changes?

**Protects:** Real urgency and comparison with the do-nothing option.

### 15 — Existing alternatives

**Ask:** What products, services, processes, competitors, substitutes, or
informal workarounds already address some part of this need?

**Protects:** Learning from the existing landscape and avoiding needless
reinvention.

### 16 — Build, buy, adapt, connect, explain, or stop

**Ask:** Why might creating something new be better than reusing, buying,
adapting, connecting, simplifying, documenting, or deliberately not proceeding?

**Protects:** A reason to build without assuming building is the answer.

## E — Operating and commercial context

### 17 — Context classification

**Ask:** Is this primarily personal, internal, public, commercial,
client-delivered, research-oriented, or some combination—and what obligations
follow?

**Protects:** Proportionate activation of later discovery branches.

### 18 — Funding or exchange

**Ask:** Who would fund, purchase, authorize, or otherwise sustain the work,
and what do they expect in return?

**Protects:** Financial and stakeholder realities without presuming a sale.

### 19 — Demand and market evidence

**Ask:** If this depends on adoption or payment, what is known about demand,
audience size, location, saturation, alternatives, and acceptable pricing?

**Protects:** Market claims from being built on unmarked assumptions.

### 20 — Sustainable delivery

**Ask:** How would people discover, access, receive, pay for, or continue using
the value, and what would it cost to deliver and support?

**Protects:** Channels, delivery, funding, cost, support, and viability.

## F — Journey and system

### 21 — Inputs and outputs

**Ask:** What enters the system or process, and what should come out?

**Protects:** The system's basic transformation.

### 22 — End-to-end journey

**Ask:** What should happen from the first trigger or entry through the
completed outcome and any necessary follow-up?

**Protects:** The complete experience rather than one screen or feature.

### 23 — Participants and dependencies

**Ask:** Which people, agents, tools, services, databases, devices, providers,
or other systems touch the journey?

**Protects:** Dependencies and third-party boundaries.

### 24 — Handoffs and preconditions

**Ask:** Where do responsibility, authority, information, or state transfer,
and what must be true before the next step begins?

**Protects:** Coordination, approvals, and hidden prerequisites.

## G — Scope, feasibility, and uncertainty

### 25 — Smallest useful proof

**Ask:** What is the smallest result that would teach us something meaningful
or provide real value without pretending the whole vision is finished?

**Protects:** An honest first proof instead of an arbitrary feature minimum.

### 26 — Scope and non-goals

**Ask:** What must be included now, what is explicitly outside the current
work, and what may be considered later?

**Protects:** Focus and visible boundaries.

### 27 — Constraints and resources

**Ask:** What limits or shapes the work—time, budget, people, skills,
technology, contracts, policy, law, legacy systems, physical conditions, or
something else?

**Protects:** Feasibility and the difference between fixed and changeable
constraints.

### 28 — Assumptions, dependencies, and unknowns

**Ask:** What are we assuming, what must another party or system provide, and
which unknowns could materially change the direction?

**Protects:** A visible research and validation queue.

## H — Data, authority, and harm

### 29 — Information and data lifecycle

**Ask:** What information or data will be collected, generated, inferred,
read, changed, shared, retained, or deleted—and which parts may be sensitive?

**Protects:** Privacy, data governance, retention, and migration discovery.

### 30 — Authority and human control

**Ask:** What may each person, agent, or system inspect, decide, recommend,
change, or communicate, and which actions require human approval?

**Protects:** Bounded authority rather than treating capability as permission.

### 31 — Failure, harm, and protection

**Ask:** What could go wrong, who or what could be harmed, how would failure be
detected, and what prevention, response, recovery, or specialist review might
be required?

**Protects:** Proportionate safety, security, privacy, legal, accessibility,
incident, and recovery work.

## I — Operation, evidence, and decision

### 32 — Operation and lifecycle

**Ask:** Who will operate, support, monitor, maintain, change, recover, and
eventually retire the result after its first release or handoff?

**Protects:** The complete operating lifecycle.

### 33 — Success and discovery decision

**Ask:** What evidence would show that the result is useful, correct, safe
enough for its context, and ready for its next stage—and what finding would
cause us to pause, redirect, or stop?

**Protects:** Acceptance, measurement, and a real discovery decision.

## Completion warning

Visiting every anchor is not the completion gate. The agent must reconcile the
answers, resolve or preserve contradictions, complete earned branches, identify
evidence limits, and obtain human review of the Pre-Build Blueprint.
