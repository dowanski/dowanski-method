# QDI Discovery Anchors

Status: Adaptive discovery guide

Purpose: Develop understanding together; use coverage without reciting a survey

## How to use the anchors

The 33 anchors identify responsibilities that discovery should address. They do
not require 33 separately spoken answers, sequential delivery, or equal depth.

For each anchor, record one outcome in the Discovery Ledger:

- `ANSWERED` — sufficient for the current decision;
- `EXPAND` — follow-up or a specialist branch is required;
- `SIDEQUEST` — evidence must be gathered outside the conversation;
- `DEFERRED` — intentionally unresolved with an owner and gate;
- `NOT_APPLICABLE` — excluded with a reason;
- `BLOCKING` — the project cannot responsibly advance without resolution.

Do not repeat a question already answered. Cite the earlier answer, verify the
interpretation if needed, and mark the overlapping anchor accordingly.

## Opening contract

Offer this once, before a long line of questioning:

“How would you like me to work with you on this project?

A) **Guide me through it** — Explain unfamiliar decisions, investigate options,
and recommend a path.

B) **Work alongside me** — Build on what I already know and help resolve gaps
and tradeoffs.

C) **Take the research and planning lead** — Develop evidence-backed proposals
and bring meaningful decisions back to me.

D) **Challenge my existing plan** — Review what I have, question assumptions,
and investigate unresolved risks.

You can combine approaches, describe another preference, or change by topic.
Would you prefer one question at a time or small groups? If unsure, I recommend
starting one at a time.”

Then ask for the idea and any existing material. Reuse answers already supplied.
Do not demand an experience rating, lengthy setup answers, or proof of technical
ability. These approaches adjust explanation and initiative, not human
authority, required evidence, QDI depth, or DM Documentation Weight. Explain
tradeoffs without condescension; challenge weak reasoning in every approach.
Keep optional setup choices out of the way until they matter.

Record the accepted current preferences in Current State, not a separate
personality document. A role change does not reopen resolved questions.

## Choose the next useful move

After enough seed context—often a few exchanges, not a fixed count—reflect the
intended result and separate what is supplied, supported, inferred, and unknown.
Early context supports hypotheses; it does not establish demand or feasibility.
Do not invent an audience, budget, capability, or requirement to avoid a question.

Choose the highest-value next move:

| Missing understanding | Agent's next move |
|---|---|
| Intent, preference, lived experience, or a commitment | Ask the human a contextual question |
| A fact in authorized project material | Inspect it; do not ask the human to transcribe it |
| An externally answerable uncertainty | Recommend proportionate research when it would affect a decision |
| A tradeoff with sufficient evidence | Explain options, recommend one, and seek a decision where needed |
| Enough support for the next bounded commitment | Recommend convergence rather than inventing more questions |

Briefly state the observation, why it matters, and the next question or
recommendation. Do not mechanically include all three in every response.
Offer plausible alternatives without steering the human through false choices.
Their freedom to correct, reject, combine, or answer outside options remains.
Do not combine questions whose answers may differ.

The example questions below are fallback wording, not a spoken checklist.
Map coverage after understanding, not by asking anchors in numeric order.
An answer or investigation can satisfy several anchors; show the useful
connection without asking for the same information again. Specialist discovery
can begin as soon as a consequential branch is earned and approved.

## Research selection

Check whether research is useful after meaningful discoveries; do not propose
it merely because an anchor was answered. First reuse sufficiently current,
authorized evidence. Human preference is not a research problem.

| Level | Use when | Expected result |
|---|---|---|
| Light Scan | A narrow fact, service capability, or initial shortlist needs checking | A sourced answer or candidates, with limits; not proof of demand |
| Standard Research | A meaningful choice requires comparison across alternatives and constraints | Traceable comparison, tradeoffs, recommendation, and unresolved questions |
| Deep Research | A consequential, interconnected or disputed uncertainty needs multiple lines of investigation | A scoped research plan, source-quality and counterevidence review, synthesis, limitations, and decision implications |

Recommend a level and why it is enough. Do not promote work to Deep Research for
prestige, or choose Light Scan when the consequence requires stronger evidence.
Research level is separate from collaboration approach, QDI discovery depth,
and DM Documentation Weight.

For example, in a fictional scheduling-service discovery:

“Your concept suggests independent practitioners and small teams as possible
audiences. They may need different onboarding. I recommend Standard Research
into their existing alternatives before we choose our first audience. Shall
we investigate that, then return to the audience decision?”

This is a hypothesis and a proposal, not a claimed research result.

A quick factual check may run within already-agreed read-only source and effort
boundaries; record its source and implications in the Ledger. Do not make a new
packet for every lookup. A dedicated research lane uses the Sidequest Template:
name the decision, scope, sources, effort/cost boundary, checkpoint, stop rule,
and exact return point, then obtain or cite explicit applicable authorization.
Approach C is not that authorization.

Deep Research may exceed thirty minutes when approved scope and resources
justify it. Time spent and source count do not prove completeness. At a
checkpoint, summarize new evidence, remaining decision gaps, and whether to
stop, narrow, or request more scope. Reaching a limit is not permission to
continue, and unresolved evidence must not be presented as a completed finding.
No tool, subscription, external contact, private access, or spending is implied.
If research tools are unavailable, report that; offer supplied sources or a
deferred research packet. Never invent searches, citations, or current facts.

## Return, synthesize, and preserve momentum

After research, explain what was learned, what remains uncertain, and what it
changes. Update evidence and affected anchor coverage once, including answers
to questions not yet asked. Distinguish a sourced fact from an interpretation
or owner-approved choice. Seek acceptance of consequential direction, not
permission to save every material note in the agreed location.

Recommend the next move rather than handing back a raw report:

“This also resolves our earlier integration question. I recommend using that
constraint in the blueprint. Pricing remains uncertain: we can investigate it
now if it changes the first version, or assign it a later decision gate.”

Return to the saved question or explicitly reconcile why the new evidence
changes that question before redirecting. Retain raw reports as supporting
evidence, not daily context. Do not multiply sidequests indefinitely; park
interesting but nonessential leads with a reason and revisit trigger.

## Enough understanding to move forward

Recommend review when intent, the next bounded outcome, scope, constraints,
dependencies, consequential risks, and acceptance evidence are coherent enough
for that commitment. Remaining uncertainties need an owner, impact, and gate;
a material blocker is not something to wave through as an “accepted unknown.”
Market research may inform a hypothesis without proving demand.

Explain what is ready and what is not, and invite the human's decision:

“We have enough understanding to review a blueprint for this first bounded
step. These remaining questions do not block it, for the reasons recorded.
I recommend moving to review. Are you ready, or is there an important concern
we have not addressed?”

This recommends CONVERGENCE and Owner Review, not implementation. Preserve the
separate Blueprint acceptance, handoff, DM setup, and build gates. Stop,
simplify, or redirect when the evidence supports that instead of building.

## A — Intent and ownership

### 01 — Seed

**Possible question:** In your own words, what idea, problem, opportunity, or change are we
exploring?

**Protects:** Original meaning before premature solution or technical framing.

### 02 — Reason and timing

**Possible question:** Why does this matter to you, and why are you considering it now?

**Protects:** Motivation, urgency, triggering events, and assumed timing.

### 03 — Desired change

**Possible question:** If this works, what should become meaningfully different?

**Protects:** The intended change rather than only the imagined artifact.

### 04 — Intent and decision authority

**Possible question:** Who holds the original intent, who makes final decisions, and whose
approval or participation can constrain the work?

**Protects:** Ownership and approval without confusing access or operational
roles with business or creative authority.

## B — Current reality and problem

### 05 — Current condition

**Possible question:** What exists today, and how is the need currently handled—with or
without a formal solution?

**Protects:** The real baseline and existing workarounds.

### 06 — Friction or opportunity

**Possible question:** Where does the current condition create friction, loss, delay, risk,
confusion, or unrealized value?

**Protects:** The experienced problem rather than the requested feature.

### 07 — Evidence of need

**Possible question:** What have you observed, measured, heard, or experienced that suggests
this is a real problem or opportunity?

**Protects:** Honest separation of evidence, interpretation, and intuition.

### 08 — Strengths worth preserving

**Possible question:** What already works, feels valuable, or should not be lost while the
condition changes?

**Protects:** Useful behavior, trust, and existing strengths.

## C — People and context

### 09 — Direct users or recipients

**Possible question:** Who will directly use, receive, experience, or depend on the result?

**Protects:** Distinction among user, buyer, owner, operator, and recipient.

### 10 — User goal

**Possible question:** What are those people trying to accomplish before, during, and after
their interaction with this idea?

**Protects:** The wider journey surrounding the proposed solution.

### 11 — Operators and affected parties

**Possible question:** Who else must operate, support, approve, supply, integrate with, or
live with the result—even without using its primary interface?

**Protects:** Indirect stakeholders and operational reality.

### 12 — Context and inclusion

**Possible question:** Which abilities, access needs, languages, devices, environments,
levels of digital comfort, or real-world conditions could change what a usable
result means?

**Protects:** Accessibility and inclusion as early discovery responsibilities.

## D — Value and alternatives

### 13 — Value

**Possible question:** What useful value should this create, and for whom?

**Protects:** Different forms of value for users, operators, owners, customers,
and affected parties.

### 14 — Cost of inaction

**Possible question:** What is likely to happen if nothing changes?

**Protects:** Real urgency and comparison with the do-nothing option.

### 15 — Existing alternatives

**Possible question:** What products, services, processes, competitors, substitutes, or
informal workarounds already address some part of this need?

**Protects:** Learning from the existing landscape and avoiding needless
reinvention.

### 16 — Build, buy, adapt, connect, explain, or stop

**Possible question:** Why might creating something new be better than reusing, buying,
adapting, connecting, simplifying, documenting, or deliberately not proceeding?

**Protects:** A reason to build without assuming building is the answer.

## E — Operating and commercial context

### 17 — Context classification

**Possible question:** Is this primarily personal, internal, public, commercial,
client-delivered, research-oriented, or some combination—and what obligations
follow?

**Protects:** Proportionate activation of later discovery branches.

### 18 — Funding or exchange

**Possible question:** Who would fund, purchase, authorize, or otherwise sustain the work,
and what do they expect in return?

**Protects:** Financial and stakeholder realities without presuming a sale.

### 19 — Demand and market evidence

**Possible question:** If this depends on adoption or payment, what is known about demand,
audience size, location, saturation, alternatives, and acceptable pricing?

**Protects:** Market claims from being built on unmarked assumptions.

### 20 — Sustainable delivery

**Possible question:** How would people discover, access, receive, pay for, or continue using
the value, and what would it cost to deliver and support?

**Protects:** Channels, delivery, funding, cost, support, and viability.

## F — Journey and system

### 21 — Inputs and outputs

**Possible question:** What enters the system or process, and what should come out?

**Protects:** The system's basic transformation.

### 22 — End-to-end journey

**Possible question:** What should happen from the first trigger or entry through the
completed outcome and any necessary follow-up?

**Protects:** The complete experience rather than one screen or feature.

### 23 — Participants and dependencies

**Possible question:** Which people, agents, tools, services, databases, devices, providers,
or other systems touch the journey?

**Protects:** Dependencies and third-party boundaries.

### 24 — Handoffs and preconditions

**Possible question:** Where do responsibility, authority, information, or state transfer,
and what must be true before the next step begins?

**Protects:** Coordination, approvals, and hidden prerequisites.

## G — Scope, feasibility, and uncertainty

### 25 — Smallest useful proof

**Possible question:** What is the smallest result that would teach us something meaningful
or provide real value without pretending the whole vision is finished?

**Protects:** An honest first proof instead of an arbitrary feature minimum.

### 26 — Scope and non-goals

**Possible question:** What must be included now, what is explicitly outside the current
work, and what may be considered later?

**Protects:** Focus and visible boundaries.

### 27 — Constraints and resources

**Possible question:** What limits or shapes the work—time, budget, people, skills,
technology, contracts, policy, law, legacy systems, physical conditions, or
something else?

**Protects:** Feasibility and the difference between fixed and changeable
constraints.

### 28 — Assumptions, dependencies, and unknowns

**Possible question:** What are we assuming, what must another party or system provide, and
which unknowns could materially change the direction?

**Protects:** A visible research and validation queue.

## H — Data, authority, and harm

### 29 — Information and data lifecycle

**Possible question:** What information or data will be collected, generated, inferred,
read, changed, shared, retained, or deleted—and which parts may be sensitive?

**Protects:** Privacy, data governance, retention, and migration discovery.

### 30 — Authority and human control

**Possible question:** What may each person, agent, or system inspect, decide, recommend,
change, or communicate, and which actions require human approval?

**Protects:** Bounded authority rather than treating capability as permission.

### 31 — Failure, harm, and protection

**Possible question:** What could go wrong, who or what could be harmed, how would failure be
detected, and what prevention, response, recovery, or specialist review might
be required?

**Protects:** Proportionate safety, security, privacy, legal, accessibility,
incident, and recovery work.

## I — Operation, evidence, and decision

### 32 — Operation and lifecycle

**Possible question:** Who will operate, support, monitor, maintain, change, recover, and
eventually retire the result after its first release or handoff?

**Protects:** The complete operating lifecycle.

### 33 — Success and discovery decision

**Possible question:** What evidence would show that the result is useful, correct, safe
enough for its context, and ready for its next stage—and what finding would
cause us to pause, redirect, or stop?

**Protects:** Acceptance, measurement, and a real discovery decision.

## Completion warning

Visiting every anchor is not the completion gate. The agent must reconcile the
answers, resolve or preserve contradictions, complete earned branches, identify
evidence limits, and obtain human review of the Pre-Build Blueprint.
