# Domain Lens — Services and Businesses

Status: Conditional  
Use for: Commercial or noncommercial offers, customer relationships, service
delivery, business concepts, and operating models

## Activate when

- someone will choose, fund, purchase, authorize, or receive an offer;
- sustainable delivery, positioning, demand, pricing, or customer trust affects
  whether the idea should proceed;
- a service process must connect promise, delivery, support, and outcome.

## Minimum coverage

1. **Offer** — What value is being offered, to whom, and in what form?
2. **Need and evidence** — What suggests the intended audience experiences or
   values the need?
3. **People and roles** — Who discovers, chooses, approves, pays, receives,
   delivers, supports, and remains accountable?
4. **Alternatives** — What competitors, substitutes, informal workarounds, and
   do-nothing options already exist?
5. **Difference** — Why would the intended audience choose this approach, and
   what must not be promised?
6. **Customer journey** — What happens from discovery through evaluation,
   agreement, onboarding, delivery, acceptance, support, renewal, or exit?
7. **Delivery system** — Which people, tools, partners, information, decisions,
   and handoffs produce the result?
8. **Commercial or funding model** — What sustains the work, what does delivery
   cost, and which assumptions remain untested?
9. **Capacity and quality** — How much can be delivered reliably, and what fails
   when demand or complexity grows?
10. **Proof and decision** — What would show enough demand, usefulness, trust,
    and delivery feasibility to proceed?

## Optional deepening prompts

- Is the buyer the user, beneficiary, approver, or a different party?
- What must someone understand before price or commitment makes sense?
- What information is needed for an accurate estimate, proposal, or decision?
- Where can expectation and actual delivery diverge?
- Which work is standardized, tailored, delegated, licensed, subscribed,
  retained, or one-time?
- Which boundaries protect both the provider and customer?
- What happens when a customer is not a good fit or the proposed result is not
  feasible?
- What service recovery, refund, revision, dispute, or offboarding behavior may
  be needed?
- What evidence supports pricing, audience size, reach, or willingness to pay?
- Which successful internal capability could responsibly become a repeatable
  service without overselling its maturity?

## Mandatory escalation signals

Escalate when the offer includes:

- payment collection, recurring billing, refunds, tax, or financial advice;
- contracts, warranties, regulated claims, licensing, or intellectual property;
- collection of customer, employee, health, financial, identity, or behavioral
  information;
- work performed by subcontractors, external agents, or third-party providers;
- a promise whose failure could cause material personal or business harm;
- automated customer communication, eligibility, ranking, pricing, or denial;
- delivery capacity that depends on one undocumented person or fragile process;
- unsupported market, performance, savings, or outcome claims.

## Evidence expectations

Useful evidence may include customer or stakeholder interviews, observed
workflow data, competitor and alternative analysis, public market sources,
pricing and cost research, small service trials, demand signals, delivery
rehearsals, policy review, and clearly labeled owner experience.

Market or customer evidence must be cited and scoped. Enthusiasm is useful
context but not independent demand validation.

## Exit condition

The blueprint can explain who receives and sustains the value, why the offer is
credible, how the complete service is delivered and supported, what the
principal commercial assumptions are, which boundaries protect the parties,
and what evidence supports proceeding or stopping.
