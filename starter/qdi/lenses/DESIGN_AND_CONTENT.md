# Domain Lens — Design and Content

Status: Conditional  
Use for: Visual identity, interaction design, user experience, editorial work,
content systems, communication, and design-to-implementation projects

## Activate when

- understanding, trust, action, or emotional response depends on presentation;
- an interface, identity, content hierarchy, or communication system is part of
  the result;
- design decisions must later transfer into implementation or ongoing content
  operations.

## Minimum coverage

1. **Audience and context** — Who encounters the work, where, on which devices,
   with which abilities and expectations?
2. **Intended understanding** — What should someone understand first, next, and
   before acting?
3. **Intended response** — What should the experience help someone feel, decide,
   or accomplish?
4. **Content and information** — What must be communicated, in what hierarchy,
   by whom, and how often does it change?
5. **Voice and vocabulary** — Which tone, terms, claims, and communication
   behaviors belong or do not belong?
6. **Visual and interaction direction** — Which references, principles, states,
   and patterns express the intended character without imitation?
7. **Accessibility and inclusion** — What reading, navigation, contrast, motion,
   input, language, cognitive, or assistive-technology needs affect the result?
8. **Authority and workflow** — Who creates, reviews, approves, edits, publishes,
   and retires design or content?
9. **Transfer to implementation** — What specification, prototype, asset,
   responsive behavior, and acceptance evidence must survive handoff?
10. **Evaluation** — How will the owner and representative readers or users know
    the direction works?

## Optional deepening prompts

- What does the intended audience already know, and what must not be assumed?
- Which message or action deserves visual priority, and which content is
  supporting evidence?
- What would make the work feel generic, inflated, confusing, inaccessible, or
  untrustworthy?
- Which parts should be calm, expressive, restrained, instructional, direct, or
  persuasive?
- How should tone change across headings, body copy, navigation, forms, errors,
  consent, help, and technical documentation?
- Which visual references carry the right essence, and which carry the wrong
  social, commercial, or functional meaning?
- What happens when content is long, absent, translated, user-generated,
  outdated, or unexpectedly complex?
- Which responsive or interaction behavior is essential rather than decorative?
- Who owns future edits, and what guardrails preserve the design system?
- Which aspects require a prototype, usability session, accessibility review,
  or exact implementation comparison?

## Mandatory escalation signals

Escalate when the work includes:

- a public or required-use experience that excludes people through inaccessible
  design or content;
- health, financial, legal, safety, eligibility, or other consequential
  communication;
- personal data, consent, account access, payment, or irreversible action;
- user-generated or AI-generated content that may be unsafe, misleading, or
  difficult to moderate;
- claims that require evidence or qualified review;
- motion, media, or interaction that can impair access or comprehension;
- several languages, jurisdictions, brands, editors, or approval authorities;
- a prototype being mistaken for verified production behavior.

## Evidence expectations

Useful evidence may include content inventory, current analytics where
authorized, representative-user research, reference analysis, information
architecture, wireframes, prototypes, design-system inspection, accessibility
evaluation, responsive captures, copy review, and design-to-code comparison.

Aesthetic preference is valid owner direction but should not be mislabeled as
user evidence or universal design truth.

## Exit condition

The blueprint can state the audience, intended understanding and action,
content hierarchy, voice, visual and interaction principles, accessibility
needs, editorial authority, implementation handoff, and evaluation standard.
