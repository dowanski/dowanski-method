# How the System Fits Together

QDI gives an idea enough definition to make responsible decisions. DM gives the
accepted work a navigable operating structure. Neither is a substitute for the
person deciding what matters or reviewing what gets built.

## QDI: build understanding before building the product

The agent begins with the human's own explanation and offers conversational
questioning. The [33 Discovery Anchors](../starter/qdi/DISCOVERY_ANCHORS.md) are
coverage responsibilities, not a mandatory questionnaire to repeat verbatim.
Known answers count; relevant gaps create follow-ups.

Conditional lenses and specialist branches explore the work that the project
actually requires. Research can interrupt discovery with a bounded sidequest,
but it preserves an exact return point. The Discovery Ledger distinguishes
accepted direction, observed facts, assumptions, inference, and uncertainty.

Convergence produces a Pre-Build Blueprint. The human reviews it, the context
cleanup, and any remaining uncertainty before accepting a transition. Finishing
the anchors alone does not establish readiness.

## DM: give each responsibility a home

```text
Main Set
└── Workstream Set, only when earned
    └── Work Packet
```

The **Main Set** holds project intent, current truth, decisions, sequence, debt,
and navigation. A **Workstream Set** is justified by an independent objective,
boundary, lifecycle, or review need—not by the desire to create more folders.
A **Work Packet** bounds one coherent outcome with authority, proof, and a stop.

Children inherit accepted parent context and may narrow permission. They cannot
silently broaden it. New uncertainty can reopen QDI at the appropriate level,
without restarting the entire project or abandoning reliable existing work.

## Match the weight to the consequence

The [foundation guide](../starter/dm-foundations/CHOOSING_A_FOUNDATION.md)
defines Light, Standard, and Governed. They differ in how responsibility is
separated and what controls the work requires. None is permission to skip
required verification. Discovery depth and documentation weight are separate
choices; extensive discovery can still lead to a small implementation.

The [adaptation guide](../starter/dm-foundations/ADAPTING_A_FOUNDATION.md)
explains how accepted truth becomes one active foundation. Unselected
foundations and unearned optional files remain outside the daily route.

## Keep status honest

Four questions remain separate:

1. **Lifecycle:** where is the work in its process?
2. **Evidence:** what has been checked, with what result and limits?
3. **Acceptance:** what has the responsible human accepted?
4. **Release:** what has actually been preserved, integrated, or made available?

A passing test is not acceptance. Acceptance is not release. A release does not
prove every live behavior. The records should make these distinctions visible.

## Let the active context get smaller

Current-state records change with reality. Accepted checkpoints and relied-upon
decisions preserve what was reviewed; later changes receive a linked successor.
Closed interviews, retries, and branches can leave the default reading route
after their useful conclusions and evidence have a durable home.

Durable IDs are conditional. Readable filenames and relative links remain the
normal navigation. An ID helps a consequential record survive moves; it does
not require every note or question to become a database row.

The system asks agents to avoid unchanged retries and unnecessary rereading,
report exact usage only from real telemetry, and reserve effort for checking
and repair. These are written controls—not measured savings or a guarantee of
compliance. [Verification and limits](verification.md).
